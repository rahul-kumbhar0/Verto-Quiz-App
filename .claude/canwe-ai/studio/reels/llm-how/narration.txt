Every answer ChatGPT gives you is written one piece at a time, and each piece is a guess. So how does a guess sound so smart? First, tokens. Your message is chopped into tokens, small chunks of text. A token is about three-quarters of a word. Next, every token becomes a vector, a long list of numbers. In GPT-3, that list is 12,288 numbers long. Then, attention. Each token looks back at every token before it and decides which ones matter. In the river bank, bank pays attention to river, not money. And that happens in 96 layers, one after another. At the end, the model scores every token it knows, all 50,257 of them, and picks a likely next one. Then it adds that token to your message and runs the whole thing again. For every single word. So where do the numbers come from? GPT-3 read 300 billion tokens and learned by guessing the next one, nudging 175 billion weights to make the right guess more likely. The idea came from an eight-author Google paper in 2017: Attention Is All You Need. So here's how to use it. It predicts what sounds right, not what is right. So give it the facts, show it an example, and ask it to check its work. Your chatbot is quietly guessing the next token, over and over, really well.

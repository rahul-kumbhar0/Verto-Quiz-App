STEP 00 · ONCE

Install the 3 tools

You need Node.js 22 or newer, FFmpeg and Claude Code (Claude in your terminal). About 10 minutes.

Mac (with Homebrew):

brew install node ffmpeg

Windows (PowerShell):

winget install OpenJS.NodeJS.LTS winget install Gyan.FFmpeg

Then Claude Code (any system):

npm install -g @anthropic-ai/claude-code

Check it all worked (close and reopen the terminal first):

node -v ffmpeg -version claude --version

node -v must say v22 or higher. The first time you run claude it asks you to log in with your Claude account (paid plan needed, see Costs).

STEP 01

The idea: steal the pattern, not the video

Open the 5 best reels in your niche from the last 2 weeks. For each, copy the first spoken line (the hook), the caption, the length and the views. Paste into Claude (claude.ai is fine for steps 1–2):

Here are 5 top reels from my niche (hook, caption, length, views). Find the pattern they share: hook type, length, structure, how they show proof, and the call to action. Then give me 5 reel ideas for MY page that use the same pattern on a topic they didn't cover. Score each idea 0–10 for: specific, stakes for my viewer, easy to SHOW on screen, and true for me. My niche: [your niche]. My audience: [who].

Pick the idea you can prove on screen. "I did X" only if you really did X.

STEP 02

The script: the 3-beat opening + one idea per line

Our structure: pain/claim (≤ 6 words) → twist with Claude → "here's how", then the steps, an honest catch, one CTA. 110–125 words = about 40 seconds.

Write a 38–42 second reel script (110–125 words) on [idea]. Structure: (1) hook, 6 words or fewer, with a number or a bold claim; (2) a twist line with Claude, 4–7 words; (3) "Here's how" or "Here are the N steps"; (4) the steps, each one short line said + shown; (5) an honest catch, said softly; (6) ONE call to action: "Comment WORD and I'll DM you [the thing]". Spoken, casual, like telling a friend. One idea per sentence, 6–14 words. No "in this video", "game-changer", "unlock". First give me 10 hooks scored 0–12 (specific, stakes, curiosity, fast, showable, true), then the script with the best hook, then 3 frame-0 headline options of 7 words or fewer.

Read it out loud. If you need a breath in the middle of a sentence, split it.

STEP 03

The voice

🎙️
ElevenLabs (what we use): model v3, one take. Add tags in square brackets for delivery: [matter of fact] on the hook, [excited] on the twist, [short pause] before the catch, [softly] on the catch, [warmly] on the CTA. Write a stressed word in CAPS. A 40-second reel costs us ~750–850 credits.
📱
Your own voice: record on your phone in a quiet room, 15 cm from your mouth. Still the most human option, and free.
💻
Free, on your laptop: an open-source text-to-speech voice such as Kokoro. Less expressive, costs nothing.

Save the file as voice.mp3. Example of a tagged line:

[matter of fact] Stop paying for video-editing courses. [excited] Claude makes every reel on this PAGE. Here are the 5 steps.
STEP 04

Create the project (with your voice in it)

npx hyperframes init my-reel --resolution portrait --audio voice.mp3 --model base.en --language en
  • 1
    What it does: makes a 1080×1920 project, copies your voice in and wires it to the timeline, transcribes it with Whisper (word timings for captions), and installs the HyperFrames skills into Claude Code.
  • 2
    First run: it downloads the Whisper model once (needs internet). If transcription fails, the project is still created; run it again on a normal connection.
  • 3
    The installer says: "Review skills before use; they run with full agent permissions." Worth 2 minutes of reading.

Then open Claude Code inside the project (a new session, so it loads the skills):

cd my-reel claude
STEP 05

Storyboard first, build second

Don't ask for the video in one go. Ask for a shot list, fix it, then build. This is the step that makes it look edited, not generated.

Using /hyperframes: here is my script (voice.mp3 is already in the project, with word timings). Before building anything, write a storyboard as a table: for every sentence give the time range, the ONE hero visual on screen (a real UI mock-up, a number, a diagram, never stock clipart), the motion that brings it in, and the transition to the next scene. Rules: frame 0 must already show a bold headline (it is the thumbnail and must work on mute); a new visual every 2–3 seconds; one accent colour; text only between 170 px and 1330 px from the top. Wait for my OK before building.

Change anything that's vague ("show AI") into something concrete ("a chat card where Claude replies with 3 chips").

STEP 06

Our look, as a prompt

This is how our reels are styled. Swap the colours for your brand.

Build it with this look. Background: graphite #0F0F11 with a faint dot grid and a soft vignette. Text: off-white #F3F1EC, one accent: terracotta #D97757 (and #F0A585 for highlights), nothing else coloured. Font: Inter, headlines weight 900, tight letter-spacing; code in JetBrains Mono. Cards: dark glass (#1B1B1F, 1.5px #2C2C31 border, 22px radius, deep soft shadow). Frame-0 headline: 2 lines, ~100px, the key word in the accent colour, rising in word by word. Section headers like "01 The idea" with the number big in the accent colour. Motion: things arrive on a fast ease-out with a tiny overshoot (power4/expo out), leave on ease-in, never linear; scene changes cut mid-motion (no slow crossfades). Keep everything inside the 90% safe area.

The cartoon presenter. Our character is our own SVG puppet. To get yours:

Draw a cartoon presenter as an inline SVG at the bottom of the frame (head + shoulders, ~600px wide, standing in from y=1330): [describe: hair, glasses, outfit, colours]. Give him poses as separate arm angles (talk, point up, point left, point right, thumbs up, shrug, wave) and a mouth that opens and closes on the voice. On each step, he moves to the side opposite the hero visual and points at it; he steps closer (scale 1.15) on the hook twist and the CTA, and slightly back (0.86) while pointing.
STEP 07

Captions: word by word, one keyword boxed

HyperFrames has ready-made caption styles. List them and install one:

npx hyperframes catalog captions npx hyperframes add caption-highlight
Using the word timings from voice.mp3, add captions at the top (y ≈ 300px), max 5 words per page, each word blurring in as it's spoken. In every page, put ONE keyword in a blue "selection box" (2px #3B82F6 border, light blue fill, two small white corner handles, like a Figma selection). Every time the word "Claude" is spoken, turn it peach #F0A585 with a soft glow and pop a small Claude spark icon right after it.
STEP 08

Motion: use the 448 ready-made blocks

Before hand-building any effect, search the registry. Blocks we found useful for reels:

Then the motion prompt (paste after the build):

Upgrade the motion: every hero visual enters on a spring (slight overshoot, settles in ~0.4s); cards deal in staggered 0.08s apart; on the hook's last word, tear the hero card in half (two clip-path halves falling apart) with a short camera shake; on the twist, a big accent burst (rays + an expanding ring + a 2-frame flash); between sections use a different cut each time (cut-the-curve, whip pan, iris from the next visual, rack focus). Nothing ever sits still for more than 2 seconds: slow drift or parallax on everything that's on screen.
STEP 09

Sound: music under, whooshes on cuts

Using /media-use: add background music under the voice (instrumental, ~130 BPM, ducks under the voice so every word is clear), a short whoosh on every scene cut, a soft pop when a card lands, and an impact on the hook's last word. Keep the voice on top. Fade the music out over the last 1.5 seconds.

Useful commands:

npx hyperframes beats # finds the music's beats so cuts can land on them npx hyperframes audio --at 12 # what sound plays where, at 12 s

Only use music you have the rights to. Trending songs: add them inside Instagram's editor instead, so Instagram handles the licence.

STEP 10

Review like an editor

npx hyperframes check npx hyperframes snapshot --at 0,3,8,15,25,35 npx hyperframes preview
  • 1
    check catches real problems. Ours flagged an empty first frame, overlapping animations, nested clips and text hidden under a shape.
  • 2
    snapshot saves PNG frames plus a contact sheet. Give the sheet back to Claude: "Look at this contact sheet. What's hard to read, cramped or boring? Fix the 3 worst things."
  • 3
    preview opens a live studio in your browser (localhost). Scrub through it with sound on, then on mute.

Revision prompts that work:

Frame 0 isn't a thumbnail yet: put the headline fully on screen at 0.0s, bigger, with the hero visual already behind it.
Between 12s and 15s nothing new happens. Add a visual that shows exactly what the voice says there.
The captions and the headline overlap at the top. Move the headline down 60px and keep 40px between them everywhere.
STEP 11

Render + the cover

npx hyperframes render -o reel.mp4

Our 8-second test rendered in 9 seconds; a 40-second reel takes a few minutes.

Make a 1080×1920 cover as a separate composition: background = a tilted, dimmed grid of my previous reel covers (files in assets/covers/), a small pill kicker on top, a 2-line headline of 5 words or fewer in 140px+ type with the keyword in the selection box, my handle under it, and the presenter pointing at the headline. Keep all text inside the middle 3:4 area (y 240–1680): that's what the profile grid shows. Render one frame to cover.png.
STEP 12

Post it (you, not Claude)

Write my Instagram caption: line 1 = "Comment WORD and I'll DM you…", then the hook, 3–5 numbered value lines, one honest line (costs / what's mine / not sponsored), a [keywords: …] line with 5 search phrases, and 4 hashtags. Then a pinned comment and a YouTube Shorts title under 60 characters.
  • 1
    Watch it twice: once with sound, once on mute. On mute, the first frame alone must say what the reel is.
  • 2
    Upload the cover as the reel cover; check the profile-grid crop.
  • 3
    Set up the DM for your keyword before you post, so the first comments get answered. (We use our own tool, 2il.in.)
  • 4
    Reply to comments in the first hour.
TROUBLESHOOTING

What went wrong for us (and the fix)

ProblemFix
Claude doesn't know HyperFramesStart a NEW Claude Code session inside the project after init, so it loads the skills. Then start prompts with "Using /hyperframes".
Render fails loading an animation library from the internetAsk: "Download gsap.min.js into assets/ and load it from there instead of the CDN."
check: "first frame empty"Put the headline on screen at 0.0s (it's your thumbnail).
check: "text occluded"Move the text, or if the overlap is on purpose (a strike line), ask Claude to mark it as intentional.
check: "overlapping tweens" / "nested structure"Paste the check output to Claude: "Fix every error in this check report."
Transcription skippedIt needs internet the first time (it downloads the Whisper model). Re-run init, or ask Claude to transcribe voice.mp3.
Captions drift from the voice"Re-time every caption word to the transcript timings; never estimate timings."

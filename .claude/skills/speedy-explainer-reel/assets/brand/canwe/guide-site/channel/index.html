11 free Claude skills for your YouTube channel. You still press upload.

The YouTube Agent skill is a free, open-source pack (5,838 ★ on GitHub, 14 Nov 2026). It adds 11 commands to Claude that write your scripts, cut lists, titles and plans. It does not edit your video and it does not post for you. Here's the link, the install line, every command, and the fine print.

STEP 01

Install it (pick one way)

Easiest: paste this into Claude.

https://github.com/Jakeschincariol/youtube-agent-skill Install this skill, then confirm /yt-script works.

In Claude Code (terminal):

git clone https://github.com/Jakeschincariol/youtube-agent-skill.git cp -r youtube-agent-skill/skills/yt-* ~/.claude/skills/

Or as a plugin, inside Claude Code:

/plugin marketplace add Jakeschincariol/youtube-agent-skill /plugin install youtube-agent

No signup, no API key. Before you install any skill, open its files on GitHub and skim them. This pack is plain text instructions plus six small Python scripts.

STEP 02

Do this first: your voice file

Every command reads one file, voice.md, so the scripts sound like you. Without it you get generic scripts. Give Claude three of your own videos and say:

Write my voice.md from these three videos and save it to ~/.claude/youtube/voice.md

No videos yet? Copy the template from the repo (templates/voice.md) and fill it in. It takes about ten minutes.

STEP 03

All 11 commands

  • 1
    /yt-script One idea → five hooks from 21 formulas, each scored, then the full spoken script with the retention beats marked.
  • 2
    /yt-package Title and thumbnail checked as a pair: too long, saying the same thing twice, too vague.
  • 3
    /yt-edit Your transcript → a cut list with timecodes: dead air, filler words, retakes. You make the cuts.
  • 4
    /yt-viral Videos in your niche, ranked by how far each one beat its own channel's normal views.
  • 5
    /yt-retention Reads your retention export from YouTube Studio: the exact second people left and what you were saying.
  • 6
    /yt-shorts Finds the Shorts inside a long video and writes a new first line for each.
  • 7
    /yt-plan A week that fits the hours you have: one main video, one cheap one, three Shorts.
  • 8
    /yt-comment Sorts your comments into four piles, drafts replies in your voice, says which one to pin.
  • 9
    /yt-seo The description, the few tags worth having, and three searches the video should win.
  • 10
    /yt-chapters Chapters from your transcript, checked against YouTube's rules so they show up.
  • 11
    /yt-audit Looks at the whole channel and ends with ONE fix, not twenty.
STEP 04

A first week (students, 5–6 hours)

📅
Day 1: /yt-plan with your real free hours, then /yt-viral for your niche (study vlogs, coding, placements).
✍️
Day 2: /yt-script on one idea. Pick from the top two hooks. Read it out loud once and fix what doesn't sound like you.
🎥
Day 3: record. Get the transcript (YouTube Studio or any free captions tool), run /yt-edit, cut by the list.
📦
Day 4: /yt-package, /yt-seo, /yt-chapters. Upload it yourself.
📈
Day 7: export retention from Studio, run /yt-retention, and use the one fix in the next video.
STEP 05

The fine print (from the author's own README)

🚫
It does not publish, and it does not edit. It writes scripts, lists and plans. You record, cut and upload. Reels that say it "runs your channel on autopilot" are overselling it.
🎯
The hook score is a rough check, not a prediction. The author tested it on 74 hooks: it spots clearly bad hooks, but it barely tells a creator's hits from their misses. A low score means look again. A high score promises nothing.
💻
Best in Claude Code (needs a paid Claude plan). In normal Claude chat you can paste a single SKILL.md, but you lose the six Python tools behind the scores and cut lists.
📄
YouTube still wants your own work. Use it to plan and write faster, then put your own ideas and voice in. Mass-produced copies of other channels get demoted.
🧪
We read every file but have not run a full channel on it. The example screens in our reel are samples that show what each command returns.

01 · THE IDEA

Three steps, on repeat

  • 1
    Your text is chopped into tokens. A token is a piece of a word. In English, one token is roughly 4 characters, or about ¾ of a word (OpenAI's rule of thumb). Common words are one token; rare or long words get split into several.
  • 2
    Each token becomes a list of numbers. The model never sees letters. Every token is turned into a long list of numbers (GPT-2 small used 768 per token) that captures how it's used. These numbers are learned during training.
  • 3
    The model scores every possible next token. It gives a probability to every token in its vocabulary — GPT-2 had 50,257 to choose from — picks one, adds it to the text, and repeats the whole thing for the next token.

That loop is why answers stream in piece by piece, and why asking the same question twice can give different answers: the model picks from probabilities, it doesn't fetch a stored answer. Without tools like web search, it's predicting from patterns it learned, not looking things up — which is also why it can sound confident and still be wrong.

💡 The numbers in the reel's bar chart were illustrative. Real probabilities depend on the model and everything before that point in the text.

02 · DO THIS (15 MIN)

See it with your own eyes

A · Watch text turn into tokens (5 min)

  1. Open OpenAI's free tokenizer: platform.openai.com/tokenizer.
  2. Paste: Why is the sky blue? — count the tokens.
  3. Now paste a long rare word (antidisestablishmentarianism), a number (2026), and the same sentence in Hindi. Which uses the most tokens? (Hint: many tokenizers spend more tokens on non-English text — that's part of why it can cost more.)

B · Same question, three answers (5 min)

Explain in two sentences why the sky is blue.

Ask this in three new chats. Write down what changed and what stayed the same. The facts usually match; the wording doesn't — that's sampling.

C · Be the model (5 min)

Cover the end of this sentence and guess the next word: "I'll have a cup of ___". Write your top 3 with a rough % for each. Then ask Claude:

For the text "I'll have a cup of", list the 5 most likely next words with a rough probability for each, and explain why the top one wins.

You just did what the model does — billions of times a day.

03 · FOR BUILDERS

Count tokens in Python

Install OpenAI's open-source tokenizer library and look at the pieces yourself:

pip install tiktoken
import tiktoken enc = tiktoken.get_encoding("gpt2") ids = enc.encode("Why is the sky blue?") print(len(ids), ids) print([enc.decode([i]) for i in ids]) print(enc.n_vocab) # size of GPT-2's vocabulary

Every AI API bills by tokens, so counting them is the first real AI-engineering skill: it decides cost and how much fits in one request.

04 · WORDS TO REMEMBER

Today's vocabulary

🧩
Token — a piece of text the model reads and writes. ≈ ¾ of an English word.
📚
Vocabulary — the full list of tokens a model knows (GPT-2: 50,257).
🔢
Embedding — the list of numbers each token becomes.
🎯
Next-token prediction — scoring every possible next token and picking one, again and again.

Your coding interview is recording more than your code.
So when someone pastes an answer from ChatGPT, how do companies actually catch it?
First, the paste.
Platforms like HackerRank log every paste and every time you switch tabs.
Forty clean lines appearing in one frame is not how humans type.
Next, your typing rhythm.
Real coders type, pause, delete and fix.
AI answers show a long pause, then a sudden burst of perfect code.
HackerRank's model even flags deleting a big block and retyping it in an odd rhythm.
Then, the comparison.
CodeSignal checks your answer against every submission on its platform and solutions posted online,
and rolls it all into one suspicion score. A human reviewer still makes the final call.
And it's rising. CodeSignal says cheating attempts jumped from sixteen percent to thirty-five percent in one year.
But what about live interviews, where the AI hides in an invisible overlay that screen share never sees?
That's where humans take over. Interviewers watch your eyes drift to the same spot, the glow in your glasses, and the long hmm while the answer loads.
In one survey, eighty-one percent of Big Tech interviewers had suspected AI cheating, and thirty-one percent had caught someone.
Their best trick is simple. One follow-up question. Why this approach? What breaks if the input doubles?
The AI wrote the code, but it can't explain it for you in real time.
That's why Google brought back an in-person round, and Amazon asks candidates to promise they won't use AI.
So use AI to prepare, not to perform. Think out loud, even when you're stuck.

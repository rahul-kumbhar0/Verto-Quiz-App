Claude + LinkedIn. The setup, and the catch.

linkedin-mcp-server connects Claude to your own LinkedIn session: it can search jobs, read postings and Easy Apply forms, read your inbox and draft messages. It's free and open source — and it comes with a real risk that this page explains before anything else.

STEP 00

The catch — in the repo's own words

⚠️ From the README: "LinkedIn's User Agreement prohibits automated access, and accounts using automated tools can be restricted or banned." And: "Use at your own risk; there is no guarantee of account safety." If your LinkedIn is your job-hunt lifeline (it is), weigh this seriously — or use the zero-risk option in step 03 instead.

This matters more for freshers than anyone: a restricted account mid-placement-season is a real loss. We're showing you the tool because it exists and you'll see it hyped elsewhere — but with the full picture.

STEP 01

The setup (if you accept the risk)

One command in your MCP client config (Claude Desktop → Settings → Developer, or Claude Code):

{ "mcpServers": { "linkedin": { "command": "uvx", "args": ["mcp-server-linkedin@latest"] } } }

First run opens a login window (or imports your browser cookies); the session is saved locally in ~/.linkedin-mcp/profile/. Then ask Claude things like:

Search LinkedIn for fresher Python jobs in Pune posted this week. Read the top 5 postings and tell me which two fit my resume best, and what each Easy Apply form will ask me.
Draft (don't send) a short, polite referral request to [name], a senior from my college at [company], for job [link/ID]. My background: [2 lines]. Keep it under 5 lines, my tone, no flattery.

STEP 02

The safety rules (how I use it)

  • ✓
    Claude reads and drafts. YOU press send. Every message, every connection request, every application — your finger, after reading it.
  • ✓
    Low volume, human pace. A handful of searches and reads in a sitting, not hundreds. Automation gets caught by volume patterns.
  • ✓
    Personal use only — your account, your job hunt. Never run it on someone else's account or for scraping lists.
  • ✗
    No mass connecting, no auto-sent messages, no spray campaigns. That's what gets accounts flagged — and it's also just spam.
  • ✗
    Don't leave it running unattended. Use it in sessions where you're reviewing what it does.

Even with all this, the risk is never zero. The repo says so; so do we.

STEP 03

The zero-risk option (no connection at all)

You can get most of the career value with LinkedIn's own official data export — no automation, nothing to ban:

  1. LinkedIn → Settings & Privacy → Data privacy → Get a copy of your data. Pick connections, messages, profile. The archive arrives by email.
  2. Give Claude the files and ask:
Here's my LinkedIn data export. 1) From my connections, list the 15 people most worth asking for a referral for [role] (company + how I know them). 2) Rewrite my headline and About section for recruiter keyword search in [field]. 3) Draft a 4-line referral message template in my tone.

Same brain, zero risk. This is the version I'd tell my younger sibling to use.

Claude Max, free for 6 months. Here's who gets it.

Anthropic's Claude for Open Source program gives open-source maintainers and contributors 6 months of Claude Max 20x, the top plan ($200/month on the web, so about $1,200 of Claude). Below: the exact criteria, how to apply, and a realistic plan for students to get there.

STEP 01

Who qualifies (you need ONE)

📦
Maintainers and library authors: 500+ dependent repos, OR 100+ dependent packages, OR 200,000+ combined monthly downloads (npm, PyPI, crates.io and others).
🏛️
Core contributors: a listed committer or maintainer on a recognised foundation or language project, like CPython, the Node.js TSC or Kubernetes.
🔁
Active contributors: 100+ pull requests merged into repos you don't own, in the last 12 months.
👥
Community builders: one of your repos has 20+ unique outside contributors with merged PRs in the last 12 months.
🛡️
Critical infrastructure: a repo you maintain has an OpenSSF criticality score of 0.4 or higher.

Source: claude.com/contact-sales/claude-for-oss (checked 28 Oct 2026). Rules can change: the official page wins.

STEP 02

How to apply

  1. Open the Claude for Open Source page and press Apply now.
  2. Link your GitHub profile and the repo or project that shows you qualify (the download page, the dependents count, the merged-PR search below).
  3. Don't fit exactly? The page says you can still apply and explain why: if something the ecosystem depends on runs on your work, say what and show the numbers.

⏳ After 6 months the free Max plan ends (Anthropic emails you first). If you already paid for a plan, it goes back to that plan and price unless you cancel; otherwise you move to the free plan. Full terms are linked on the page.

STEP 03

The student path: 100 merged PRs in a year

Most students won't have a library with 200,000 downloads. The one criterion you can work towards is the active contributor one: 100+ pull requests merged into other people's repos in 12 months. That's about 2 a week. It's serious work, but every merged PR also shows up on your GitHub, which helps your job hunt whether or not you apply.

Check your count any time (replace YOUR_USERNAME):

https://github.com/search?q=is%3Apr+is%3Amerged+author%3AYOUR_USERNAME+-user%3AYOUR_USERNAME&type=pullrequests

This lists your merged PRs, excluding repos under your own account.

STEP 04

A plan for your first 10 PRs

Week 0 · Set up (1 evening)

  • A clean GitHub profile: real name or handle, photo, one line about you.
  • Learn the loop once: fork → clone → branch → commit → push → open a PR. GitHub's own guide: Contributing to a project.

Where to find issues

  • Open "good first issue" issues on GitHub (add language:python or language:javascript to the search).
  • goodfirstissue.dev and up-for-grabs.net: curated beginner issues.
  • The libraries you already use (your requirements.txt or package.json): you know them, so you spot real problems.

PRs 1–3 · Docs

Broken links, outdated install steps, a missing example you needed yourself. Small, real, and maintainers like them.

PRs 4–6 · Tests

Find a function with no test and add one. Run the project's test command before you push.

PRs 7–10 · Small bugs

Pick issues labelled good first issue or help wanted. Comment first ("Can I take this?") so two people don't work on the same thing.

Rules that get PRs merged

  • Read CONTRIBUTING.md first and follow the project's style.
  • One change per PR, linked to the issue, with a clear title and a short "what and why".
  • Reply to review comments quickly and politely.
  • Real fixes, not spam. Typo-farming and AI-generated PRs you didn't test get closed, and maintainers remember names.

Use Claude as your mentor (not your ghostwriter)

I'm a student making my first open-source contribution. Here is the issue and the relevant files (pasted below). Explain what the code does, where the fix probably goes, and how to test it. Don't write the full fix for me: give me hints, then review my change when I paste it.
